# Proposed editorial standards

Status: initial proposal for maintainer review. These standards describe the intended publication process; they do not certify that all existing live content has been audited against them.

## Scripture and sources

- Use the King James Bible for Scripture quotations and references.
- Provide book, chapter, and verse references for biblical claims.
- Preserve the wording of a quotation and identify any omissions clearly.
- Distinguish what a passage states from an editorial inference drawn from it.
- Identify historical or geographical sources separately when they supply information beyond the biblical text.

## Narrative and biographical detail

Narratives should make the biblical account accessible without presenting invented dialogue, private thoughts, or reconstructed events as Scripture. Any imaginative reconstruction should be identified as editorial interpretation.

Region, occupation, family relationships, and other biographical details should have supporting references. An uncertain identification should remain uncertain. Ages, physical descriptions, and similar details selected for an artistic character model should be identified as artistic choices rather than established historical facts.

## Visual storytelling

Portraits and gallery scenes are modern artistic interpretations. They are not authenticated likenesses or photographs of biblical events.

For AI-generated work, record the prompt and available generation details, the identity reference used, significant edits, and the human review decision. Record only details actually known. Visual review should consider continuity, anatomy, materials, setting, and the claims an image might imply.

Publish approved assets and references that are intended for public inspection. Describe the source and permitted use of third-party material when known; do not infer reuse permission from its presence in this repository.

## Review and revisions

Each profile snapshot should identify its status, review date, supporting references, and relationship to the live page. Keep enough revision history for a reader to understand meaningful corrections and editorial changes.

Separate completed work from plans. A generated image, seeded account, or drafted narrative should not by itself be described as a completed profile.
